## What Hermes is actually using

Hermes has two separate WhatsApp integrations:

### 1. Hermes WhatsApp bot — Baileys

This is what we configured.

Architecture:

```text
Hermes gateway
    ↓
Node.js bridge
    ↓
Baileys library
    ↓
WhatsApp Web protocol
    ↓
Linked personal WhatsApp account
```

The Hermes bridge:

- Displays a QR code.
- Links a WhatsApp account as a WhatsApp Web device.
- Stores WhatsApp session credentials locally.
- Maintains a WebSocket connection.
- Sends messages to WhatsApp JIDs such as:

```text
919597423220@s.whatsapp.net
```

Internally, Hermes sends to its local bridge endpoint:

```text
POST http://127.0.0.1:3000/send
```

The bridge then calls Baileys’ WhatsApp Web socket and sends the message.

This does **not** use:

- Meta Graph API
- WhatsApp Business Cloud API
- Meta message templates
- Meta Business billing
- Official WhatsApp Business Platform infrastructure

Hermes’s official documentation describes this as an unofficial Baileys bridge with account-ban risk:

- [Hermes WhatsApp Baileys documentation](https://hermes-agent.nousresearch.com/docs/user-guide/messaging/whatsapp)
- [Hermes WhatsApp Cloud API documentation](https://hermes-agent.nousresearch.com/docs/user-guide/messaging/whatsapp-cloud)
- [Baileys GitHub repository](https://github.com/whiskeysockets/Baileys)

## Can you use Baileys for 400–2,000 marketing messages per day?

Technically, a custom Node.js service could use the same Baileys approach.

However, I do **not** recommend using it for this purpose, and I would not help build a bulk-marketing sender around it.

Reasons:

1. **It is not an official WhatsApp Business API.**
2. **Bulk or automated messaging is specifically discouraged by the Baileys project.**
3. A high-volume initial-outreach pattern is likely to trigger:
   - WhatsApp account restrictions
   - Device logout
   - Message delivery failures
   - Permanent number bans
4. It bypasses the official WhatsApp Business controls and billing model.
5. You would lose reliable:
   - Template approval
   - Campaign quality monitoring
   - Opt-out enforcement
   - Business verification
   - Official delivery guarantees
   - Meta support and appeals
6. If the recipients did not explicitly opt in, it creates a spam/compliance risk even if the messages are technically delivered.

The fact that Hermes can send a message through Baileys does not make Baileys suitable for commercial outreach.

## Important distinction

Hermes Baileys is appropriate for:

```text
A dedicated bot number
Inbound conversations
Customer replies after they initiate contact
Internal testing
Small conversational workflows
```

It is not appropriate for:

```text
400–2,000 unsolicited first-contact messages per day
Marketing campaigns
Multi-recipient bulk outreach
Trying to avoid Meta message charges
```

## Recommended production architecture

Keep your CRM connected to the official Cloud API:

```text
CRM
 ↓
Campaign/consent service
 ↓
Message queue
 ↓
Meta-approved WhatsApp template
 ↓
WhatsApp Cloud API
 ↓
Webhook for delivery/read/reply events
 ↓
CRM status update
```

Implement these controls:

### 1. Consent ledger

Store:

```text
phone number
consent source
consent timestamp
consent wording/version
country
marketing consent status
opt-out timestamp
```

Do not send marketing messages unless the recipient has an appropriate opt-in.

### 2. Approved templates

Use Meta-approved templates for initial outreach. Keep separate templates for:

- Lead follow-up
- Appointment invitation
- Product announcement
- Renewal reminder
- Event invitation

Do not try to classify a marketing message as utility merely to reduce cost. Meta determines whether the template category is appropriate.

### 3. Queue-based delivery

Use a queue such as:

```text
CRM → Redis/RabbitMQ/Azure Service Bus → sender workers → Meta API
```

Each message should have:

```text
campaign_id
recipient
template_name
template_variables
status
attempt_count
provider_message_id
error_code
sent_at
delivered_at
read_at
```

### 4. Rate and quality controls

At minimum:

- Per-campaign rate limit
- Per-number suppression list
- Duplicate prevention
- Invalid-number filtering
- Delivery failure threshold
- Automatic campaign pause
- Complaint/negative-response monitoring
- STOP/unsubscribe handling
- Human review for message copy

### 5. Webhook processing

Your CRM should process Meta webhook events:

```text
sent
delivered
read
failed
replied
```

A reply from a user opens the customer-service conversation window. Within that window, service replies can be handled differently from outbound marketing templates. Meta’s current pricing and policy documentation should be used for the exact rate and category applicable to your country and account:

- [Meta WhatsApp pricing](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing)
- [WhatsApp Business Platform pricing](https://whatsappbusiness.com/products/platform-pricing/)
- [WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy](https://whatsappbusiness.com/policy/)

## How to reduce the cost legitimately

The practical options are:

1. **Improve opt-in quality** so messages are sent only to likely responders.
2. **Use Click-to-WhatsApp ads or web forms** to collect consent and initiate conversations.
3. **Use utility templates where they genuinely qualify**, not as relabeled marketing.
4. **Move non-conversational campaigns to email, SMS, RCS, or push notifications** where appropriate.
5. **Use a Meta-approved Business Solution Provider** if it offers better tooling or commercial terms—not to bypass Meta fees.
6. **Ask Meta or your BSP about volume pricing and account-level commercial terms** for 2,000+ daily messages.
7. **Measure delivered conversion**, not just sent-message cost. A cheaper channel with a high ban or failure rate can cost more operationally.

Use the technologies like this:
